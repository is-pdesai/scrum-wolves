03 · Diet

Built to hunt big game.

Wolves are obligate carnivores — pure meat-eaters. They are big, fast, and built for the chase, but they win by working together more than by being individually overwhelming.

What's on the menu

The bulk of a wolf's diet is hoofed mammals (ungulates) — animals far larger than any single wolf. The exact prey depends on territory, but the pattern is the same: take down something big, share it across the pack.

How often they eat

A wolf can go many days between meals, then gorge when a kill comes through. Adult wolves average 5–7 lb of meat per day across the year, but a single feeding can put down 20+ lb at once — closer to the way a lion eats than a dog.

How a hunt works

  1. Locate. Scent and tracks. Wolves can smell prey from over a mile away.
  2. Stalk. The pack approaches against the wind, fanning out.
  3. Test. Provoke a chase to identify weak, sick, or young animals — the only realistic targets.
  4. Pursue. Bursts of 35+ mph; sustained 5 mph for hours.
  5. Take down. Larger prey is hamstrung first, then suffocated by a throat or muzzle hold.
  6. Share. Breeding pair eats first; then pups; then yearlings.
