04 · Habitat

Found across the Northern Hemisphere.

Gray wolves once ranged across more of the planet than almost any other land mammal. Today they hold on across northern North America, Europe, and Asia — anywhere there's enough wild prey and enough room.

Where on Earth

Wolves live in Canada, Alaska, the northern Lower 48, Mexico, Greenland, Scandinavia, the Carpathians, the Balkans, Russia, Mongolia, the Indian subcontinent, and the Middle East. They are absent from the Southern Hemisphere except where dingoes (sometimes classed as a wolf subspecies) live in Australia.

What surroundings do they need?

Wolves are habitat generalists — they don't need a particular tree or terrain. What they need is:

  • Large wild prey. Enough deer, elk, moose, or caribou to feed a pack year-round.
  • Space. Pack territories run from 50 sq mi (prey-rich forest) to over 1,000 sq mi (sparse tundra).
  • Distance from people. Wolves tolerate roads and farms surprisingly well, but they need somewhere quiet to den.
  • Water. Drinking water, and ideally a way to cool off in summer.

Beyond that, they will live in boreal forest, temperate forest, mountain, prairie, tundra, semi-desert, and Arctic ice. The only major terrestrial biome they avoid is true tropical rainforest.

Where does a wolf actually live?

For most of the year, a wolf doesn't have a "home" — the pack moves through its territory, sleeping wherever the day ends. Outside of denning season, they bed down on bare ground, in snow hollows, or under deadfall.

The den

From late spring through summer, the breeding female uses a den to give birth and raise pups. Dens are typically:

  • Underground, dug into a hillside or stream bank — sometimes 6–14 ft deep with a turning chamber at the end.
  • Repurposed from old fox, badger, or coyote dens.
  • Inside a hollow log, rock crevice, or shallow cave, when ground is too frozen or rocky to dig.

Once pups are mobile (around 8 weeks), the pack moves them to a rendezvous site — a clearing where pups stay while adults hunt. By fall, the pups travel with the pack and the den is abandoned.
