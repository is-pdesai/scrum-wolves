🍽️ Diet & Hunting

What does a cobra eat — and how does it catch its prey?

What Cobras Eat

🐭

Rodents

Mice, rats, and other small mammals are a staple meal for most cobra species.

🐸

Frogs & Toads

Amphibians are easy prey near ponds and rice paddies.

🐦

Birds & Eggs

Small birds and eggs raided from nests are a tasty bonus meal.

🦎

Lizards

Various lizard species are a common part of a cobra's diet.

🐍

Other Snakes!

King Cobras are ophiophagous — snake-eaters. They even tackle venomous pythons!

How Cobras Hunt

1

👅 Detect with Tongue

Cobras flick their forked tongue to "smell" the air, detecting prey from hundreds of metres away using their Jacobson's organ.

2

🥷 Ambush or Stalk

Some cobras lie in wait; others actively track prey using heat, scent trails, and movement.

3

💉 Strike & Inject Venom

They strike fast, biting and injecting neurotoxic venom through hollow fangs. The venom attacks the nervous system, causing paralysis.

4

🍽️ Swallow Whole

Once prey is paralysed, the cobra swallows it headfirst and whole. Their flexible jaw unhinges to fit meals larger than their head!

⚡
Super Cool Fact! After one big meal, a cobra can go weeks or even months without eating again. They have an incredibly slow metabolism!

🥚 Mating & Reproduction

How baby cobras come into the world

💑

Finding a Mate

Male cobras compete for females by wrestling — they push and try to pin each other down. No biting allowed! The winner earns the right to mate.

📅

Mating Season

Indian cobras mate between January and April. King Cobras mate January to March. Males can sense female pheromones from huge distances.

🥚

Laying Eggs

Cobras are oviparous — egg layers! A female lays between 10 and 45 eggs at a time, depending on the species.

🏠

The King's Nest ⭐

King Cobras are unique! They are the ONLY snake species that builds a nest — pushing leaves and twigs into a mound — then guards it fiercely until the eggs hatch.

⏱️

Incubation

Cobra eggs take 60–80 days to hatch. The mother stays close to keep eggs warm. She leaves just before hatching so she doesn't eat her own young!

🐍

Baby Cobras (Hatchlings)

Baby cobras are completely independent from birth — and fully venomous from day one. A newborn cobra can already spread its hood and deliver a dangerous bite!

🤯
Mind-Blowing! Baby cobras are venomous the moment they crack out of the egg — there's zero learning period. They are born ready!

Types of Environments

🌿

Tropical Rainforests

Dense, humid forests with rich biodiversity — perfect for King Cobras who hunt large prey.

🌾

Agricultural Land & Rice Paddies

Many cobras live near farmland where rodents are plentiful. This often brings them into contact with humans.

🏜️

Grasslands & Scrublands

Open areas offer warmth and plenty of small prey. Cobras bask in the sun to warm their bodies.

🏘️

Near Human Settlements

Indian cobras are surprisingly comfortable near villages and towns — wherever there are rats, a cobra might follow!

🌡️
Cold-Blooded! Cobras are ectotherms — they rely on the sun to regulate body temperature. That's why you often spot them basking on warm rocks or open ground in the morning.

🐍 Behavior

How cobras act, defend themselves, and interact with their world

🪬

The Hood Spread

When threatened, a cobra lifts its front body off the ground and fans its hood by spreading its elongated neck ribs. The message is clear: "Back off — or else!"

📢

Hissing

Cobras force air out of their lungs to produce a loud hiss. The King Cobra's hiss is so deep it sounds like a dog's growl — very different from other snakes!

💦

Venom Spitting ⭐

Spitting cobras fire venom with sniper-like accuracy — targeting a predator's eyes from up to 8 feet away! It causes intense pain and can cause blindness if not washed out quickly.

🌙

Activity Patterns

Most cobras are crepuscular — most active at dawn and dusk. Some are daytime hunters, others prefer the cool of night, depending on the species and weather.

🧍

Solitary Animals

Cobras prefer to live alone. They only come together to mate. Two cobras in the same area will generally avoid each other — unless one is a King Cobra looking for lunch!

🎵

Snake Charming — The Truth!

Cobras cannot hear music — they're nearly deaf to airborne sound! They follow the movement of the charmer's flute, not the music. It's all about the sway, not the tune!

👁️
No Eyelids! Cobras never blink. A transparent scale called a brille (German for "spectacles") covers each eye, giving them their famous intense, unblinking stare.

👅

Forked Tongue

Picks up scent particles from the air and transfers them to the Jacobson's organ in the roof of the mouth. The two tips tell the cobra which direction the smell is coming from!

🪬

The Hood

Formed by elongated neck ribs that fan outward under the skin. When relaxed, you can't even see it — but the cobra can spread it in less than a second!

💉

Venom System

Venom glands sit behind the eyes. When the cobra bites, jaw muscles squeeze the glands, forcing venom through hollow fangs. Cobra venom is mainly neurotoxic — it paralyses the nervous system.

👁️

Eyes & The Brille

Instead of eyelids, each eye is covered by a transparent scale called a brille. Cobras cannot blink or close their eyes. Their vision is tuned to detect movement rather than fine detail.

🦷

Flexible Jaws

The lower jaw is connected by a stretchy ligament — not fused like in humans. This lets cobras swallow prey far wider than their own head! Both halves of the jaw move independently.

🐾

Scales & Skin Shedding

Scales are made of keratin — the same material as your fingernails! Cobras shed their entire skin as they grow. Just before shedding (ecdysis), their eyes turn cloudy blue.

📏

Size

Most cobras: 4–6 feet. The King Cobra holds the record — the longest ever measured was 18.8 feet (5.7 m)! King Cobras can raise the front third of their body and look a person in the eye.

❤️

Internal Organs

All organs are long and narrow to fit the snake's shape. Cobras have a 3-chambered heart, mostly one functional lung, and ribs running nearly the full length of their body!

🔄
Shedding Season! Young, fast-growing cobras can shed their skin every few weeks. Adults shed a few times per year. The shed skin comes off in one complete piece — including the eye covers!

What Makes a Snake a "Cobra"?

🪬 Can spread a hood when threatened
💉 Member of the Elapidae family (fixed front fangs)
☠️ Produces potent, mainly neurotoxic venom
🌡️ Cold-blooded (ectotherm) reptile

Note: "Cobra" is a common name, not a strict scientific category. King Cobras belong to a different genus (Ophiophagus) but are still called cobras because of their hood-spreading ability and Elapidae family membership.
